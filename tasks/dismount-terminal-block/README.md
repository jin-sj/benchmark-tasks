# Take one terminal block off a wired DIN-rail strip in a control panel with a screwdriver

A control panel's mounting plate, as panel builders wire it: a DIN rail between two wiring ducts, a strip of push-in
terminal blocks on the rail, and every beige block wired into the ducts. The robot must take the blue block off the
rail with a flat screwdriver and put it in a tray, leaving the beige blocks latched where they are.

A latched block can't be pulled, pushed or slid off. As with real push-in terminal blocks, it comes off with a
flat-blade screwdriver in its release slot:
1. put the blade into the release slot at the block's near end (toward the arms), between the wired neighbours;
2. lever the sprung foot outward, so its tooth comes out from under the rail's near lip;
3. tip the near end up a few degrees;
4. slide the block a few millimetres toward the far side, so its rigid hook comes out from under the far lip;
5. lift it off, past the ducts, to the tray.

Who does this: electricians (O*NET 47-2111.00, about 0.8M US workers; task line "Connect wires to circuit breakers,
transformers, or other components") and electrical and electronic equipment assemblers (51-2028.00, panel and
control-cabinet builders) add, remove and rearrange terminal blocks on DIN rails whenever a control panel is built,
modified or rewired. The difficulty is the tool work at a 2.4 x 4.8 mm slot and the constrained tip-slide-lift of a
block held by a rigid hook, with wired neighbours and ducts around it that must not be disturbed.

## Spacing

On a real strip the blocks touch. The blue block's two beige neighbours here stand 10 mm from its faces, because a
YAM fingertip is 6.3 mm thick (10 mm thick from 12 mm above its tip): the right hand has to put a fingertip on each
side of the blue block to take it off. With the strip packed, we tried gripping the block by its two ends instead
(the fingers closing across the rail): the fingers are 26 mm wide along the rail, so they meet the neighbours' ends
and their wires, which cross the band between the block ends and the ducts. Every other block of the strip is
packed. The screwdriver work, the ducts and the neighbours' wires are as on a packed strip.

## Run and score a trial

Environment: yam_arena (Isaac Lab-Arena 0.3.1, Isaac Sim 6.1, PhysX at 240 Hz, GPU). Run at 20 Hz control with object
jitter off (the blocks start latched and must start exactly on the rail):

    inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena -E task=task.py -E control_hz=20 -E object_jitter=0 \
        --policy <policy> --scorer task_score --max-steps 2400 \
        --instruction "Take the blue terminal block off the DIN rail with the screwdriver and put it in the tray, and leave the beige blocks where they are."

`task_score` gives 1.0 when the episode ends in success, otherwise `score()` at the last step. One fixed layout, no
seeds needed. Runtime: about 1-4 minutes of simulator start-up, then a full 2,400-step trial takes about 5-8 minutes.

Scripted demo (GPU physics; each run is the first episode of a fresh simulator process):

    python demo.py                          # full: result.json, steps.jsonl, one video per camera
    python demo.py --variant held --no-video
    python demo.py --variant lifted --no-video
    python demo.py --variant lever_only --no-video
    python demo.py --variant no_lever --no-video

## The scene

All positions from the arm bases' midline: x forward, y to the left.

- **Mounting plate** (fixed): galvanised steel, 240 x 280 x 2.5 mm, lying flat on the table, its centre at x 0.305 m,
  y -0.04 m. The plate lies flat as panel builders wire it on the bench; standing upright in an enclosure, the same
  strip is out of the arms' reach.
- **Rail** (screwed to the plate): steel TS35 x 7.5, 240 mm, running left to right, its centre at x 0.30 m, y -0.04 m.
- **Wiring ducts** (screwed to the plate): grey PVC, slotted walls, 40 x 40 mm, 280 mm long, lids on, one along each
  side of the rail, each 30 mm from the block ends (the near duct from x 0.195 to 0.235 m, the far duct from 0.367 to
  0.407 m).
- **Terminal strip:** 15 polyamide push-in terminal blocks between two zinc end brackets (8 mm): the blue block (its
  centre at y -0.06 m, 2 cm right of the rail's centre), a beige block 10 mm from each of its faces, and beyond each of
  those six more beige blocks packed 0.2 mm apart. Each block: 6.2 mm thick (along the rail), housing 64.8 mm long
  across the rail (72.4 mm with the release tab), 36 mm tall (29.1 mm above the lips), 9.5 g. The hook is at the far
  end (away from the arms), the sprung foot and its release slot at the near end. In the simulation the blue block and
  its two neighbours are jointed blocks (scored); the twelve further blocks are fixed to the rail.
- **Wires:** every beige block has a black 1.5 mm2 PVC-insulated stranded wire (3.0 mm) in the wire entry in each end
  (11.25 mm above the lips), running straight across to the duct on that side, through its wall slot. The blue block's
  two wire entries are empty: its wires have already been disconnected.
- **Screwdriver and holder:** a flat-blade electrician's screwdriver (blade 4.0 x 0.8 mm, ISO 2380, 10 mm long; 3 mm
  shaft, 30 mm from the tip to the handle; 20 mm handle 45 mm long; 75 mm overall; 60 g), standing handle up in a black
  plastic holder (40 x 40 mm, 50 mm tall, a 9 mm hole), its centre at x 0.16 m, y 0.08 m.
- **Tray:** an open black plastic tray, inside 170 x 64 mm, walls 3 mm thick and 12 mm high, its centre at x 0.18 m,
  y -0.225 m (in front of the right arm, beside the plate), empty.

## The latch

The block is this task's own design. All dimensions in the block's frame: x across the rail from its centreline
(toward the arms negative), z up from the lip tops (the rail's lips are 17.5 mm from its centreline at their edges).

| feature | dimensions |
| --- | --- |
| housing | x -27.8 to +37.0 mm (64.8 mm), top at z 29.1 mm, 6.2 mm thick |
| hook (far end) | rigid, part of the housing; its tooth reaches 3.5 mm in under the far lip, 0.3 mm below the lip's underside |
| sprung foot (near end) | slides outward (toward the arms) up to 2.5 mm; its tooth reaches 2.0 mm in under the near lip, 0.3 mm below the lip's underside, so it clears the lip's edge after 2.0 mm; 5.9 mm wide (0.15 mm inside each face) |
| no sliding off | the foot's outer face stands 0.3 mm outside the near lip's edge: with the foot under its lip, a push toward the far side stops after 0.3 + 2.5 = 2.8 mm, short of the hook's 3.5 mm |
| foot spring | 1.0 N/mm, no preload: **2.0 N at 2.0 mm** (clear of the lip), pushed outward at the foot |
| cheek (housing) | x -30.0 to -27.8 mm, z 0.5 to 9.0 mm |
| **release slot** | x -32.4 to -30.0 mm (**2.4 mm**) by 4.8 mm along the rail, between the housing's side walls (0.7 mm each); its floor (on the foot) at z 0, open at the top at z 9.0: **9 mm deep** |
| tab (on the foot) | an upright x -35.4 to -32.4 mm (3.0 mm), z -1.0 to 9.0 mm, and the slot's floor, x -32.4 to -27.8 mm, z -1.0 to 0 |
| screwdriver fit | the 4.0 x 0.8 mm blade goes in from above with 1.6 mm of play across the slot and 0.8 mm along it; pushed or levered outward it pushes the tab, and with it the foot, out |
| seat pads | two pads under the housing press on the lips, 3 N/mm (the clamp of a latched block): printed flexure leaves |
| wire entries | 3.2 mm square, 8 mm deep, in both end faces at z 11.25 mm |

The screwdriver is used as a lever: blade in the slot, the handle tipped toward the block about the slot's top edge
on the cheek and pushed toward the arms, so the blade's tip pushes the tab (and the foot) out.

## Building the real block (build/)

`python build/make_latch.py` writes `build/housing.stl`, `build/foot.stl` and the section drawing
`build/latch_drawing.png` from the same constants as the simulated block (`tb_parts.py` as `task.py` sets it up). One
difference: the simulation's rail lips are 2 mm thick, a real rail's 1 mm, so in the printed parts the teeth sit 1 mm
higher (still 0.3 mm under the lip). Both parts are outlines extruded along the rail in a few layers; they print lying
on a broad face (the STL's Z is along the rail), the layers touching face to face (slicers merge them).

Per block: one housing and one foot (printed); one hardened steel dowel pin 1.5 x 6 mm (ISO 8734); one compression
spring of music wire, 0.3 mm wire, 3.0 mm outer diameter, 5.6 mm free length, 4 active coils (6 in all, ends closed
and ground), rate 1.0 N/mm (solid length 1.8 mm). The strip needs 15 blocks (1 blue, 14 beige).

Printing: PA12 by selective laser sintering (any online print service), or a tough engineering resin on an MSLA
printer (0.05 mm layers); dyed or painted blue or beige.

Assembly (a few minutes per block):
1. Check the two seat pads (the bumps under the housing, each on a flexure leaf): 300 g on a pad presses it in 1.0 mm
   (3 N/mm, within 20 %); sand the leaf's top if it is stiffer.
2. Put one end of the spring on the boss on the foot's head.
3. From below, push the foot's tongue up between the two guide walls under the housing's near end (the tab outside
   the release slot, the slot's floor under the cheek); press the foot toward the cheek, compressing the spring, so its
   free end drops into its seat (the pocket in the cheek's inner face); let go.
4. Line the tongue's slot up with the holes in the guide walls and press the pin through, flush with both faces.
5. Check: the foot rests against the pin; pushed outward it stops after 2.5 mm; 200 g hung from the tab by a thread
   over the table's edge moves it about 2 mm (2.0 N at 2.0 mm).
6. On the rail: hook the far end under the far lip and press the near end down until the foot clicks.

The panel: any flat steel or aluminium plate of the size above, a TS35 x 7.5 rail and two end brackets, two 40 x 40 mm
slotted PVC wiring ducts with lids, 28 pieces of 1.5 mm2 stranded PVC wire pushed into the beige blocks' wire entries
and laid into the ducts; the rail and ducts screwed to the plate, the plate clamped to the table.

## Scoring

At the end of the episode:

| credit | condition |
| --- | --- |
| 1.0 | success: the blue block **released and resting on the tray's floor**, still, held for 1 s in a row, with the two beige blocks beside it undisturbed |
| 0.75 | the blue block in the tray (its centre inside the tray's inner walls, its lowest point below their tops), released or still held |
| 0.5 | the blue block **off the rail**: its base 20 mm or more above the lips, or its centre more than 47.5 mm from the rail's centreline or beyond its ends |
| 0.25 | the blue block's **sprung foot released**: its tooth 2.0 mm or more out past its latched position, measured against the rail, or the block's near end lifted 6.5 mm or more off the lip |
| 0 | otherwise: in particular a block still latched by its foot, however far it was shifted or tipped |

- **Undisturbed:** each of the two beige blocks beside the blue one still latched (base within -0.5..+0.8 mm of the
  lip tops at both lip edges, centred within 1 mm, foot in, square and level within 3 degrees) and within 1 mm of where
  it started along the rail. If one was disturbed, success is impossible and the score is capped at 0.25. (The rail is
  screwed down; the scorer still checks it moved less than 10 mm and turned less than 3 degrees.)
- **Released and resting on the tray's floor:** no part of the robot touching the block (the robot's contact force on
  the block under 0.05 N), and its lowest point within 2 mm of the tray floor's top, for the whole 1 s.
- **Still:** under 10 mm/s and 0.5 rad/s, from the block's pose change over the last control step.

Real-trial checks, with a ruler, a caliper and the trial video or a photo: the blue block lies in the tray with the
gripper open and clear of it (video); or it is held clear of the rail (ruler: 20 mm under its base); or its foot's
tab stands 2.0 mm or more further out from the rail's near lip than the neighbours' tabs (caliper), or its near end
6.5 mm or more above the lip (ruler); whether the beige blocks moved along the rail (pencil marks on the rail beside
them, 1 mm) or came loose (pressing the near end down: a latched block doesn't move).

No unintended strategy scores: the latched block can't be pulled, pushed or levered off the rail without opening its
sprung foot (its hook is rigid), the foot can only be opened through the release slot, and no shift or tilt of a
latched block earns credit. Prying the beige blocks disturbs them (capped at 0.25).

## The demo

The script plans from the known starting layout and the arms' joint readings only (the screwdriver's blade tip from
the left hand's measured pose and the grasp; the block where the layout puts it); simulated object poses go only to
the log.

Left arm (the screwdriver):
1. takes the screwdriver from its holder by the handle (fingers closing across the rail) and lifts it out;
2. leans it 6 degrees away from the block (handle toward the arms);
3. brings the blade over the release slot and lowers it in; if it lands on the slot's edge (the hand stops high), it
   lifts and tries 0.4 mm to either side;
4. levers: tips the handle 10 degrees toward the block about the slot's top edge while pushing the hand 20 mm toward
   the arms (the command leading the soft arm), so the blade pushes the tab and the foot out from under the lip;
5. draws the screwdriver up 8 mm (command): the near end comes up with it and the foot's tooth comes to rest on the
   lip's edge;
6. takes the blade out and moves away.

Right arm (the block), once the screwdriver is out of the way:
1. comes in high, leaning 20 degrees, and grips the block across its broad faces 5 mm from its middle, its fingertips
   in the 10 mm gaps beside it, 11 mm deep;
2. tips the near end up along a 15 degree arc about the hook (command);
3. slides the block along its tipped length toward the far side (15 mm command) until the hook is out;
4. lifts it clear, carries it to the tray and lets go just above the floor; moves away.

## How it is built

yam_arena's task files only offer single primitives and library assets and have no extension point for jointed
parts. `tb_parts.py` is the task's own mechanism; nothing in yam_arena is modified: `task.py` calls
`tb_parts.install()`, which wraps `yam_arena.scene.task_object` and `object_states` when that module is imported
(object jitter must be 0: the blocks start latched). Parts are spawned from USD files written to a temporary folder.

- **Block:** an articulation, the geometry in *The latch*. The housing's collision is two boxes; the hook is part of
  the housing; the sprung foot is its own link on a prismatic joint across the rail, sprung inward (1 N/mm, up to
  2.5 mm out), carrying the tooth, the sole and the release slot's tab as convex pieces; two sprung seat pads press on
  the lips. The build features (guide walls, pin, the foot's head and tongue) are looks.
- **Panel:** one fixed body with the plate, the rail and end brackets, the ducts, the twelve further blocks (housings
  collide) and the wires (3 mm square colliders under round looks).
- **Screwdriver:** a free rigid body; **holder** and **tray:** fixed.

## Assets

No third-party assets: every part is this task's own geometry, built in code (`tb_parts.py`, `build/make_latch.py`);
no meshes, scans or CAD files from others are used.

## Fidelity

- **The latch:** the sprung foot is a 1 N/mm slider (release 2.0 N at the lip's edge), standing in for the printed
  block's steel spring. The hook is rigid.
- **Collision shapes:** the block's housing two boxes, plus convex pieces for the feet, the slot's cheek, side walls and
  tab; the wires 3 mm square rods, fixed (straight from block to duct).
- **The rail's lips** are 2 mm thick (1 mm on a real rail): at 240 Hz a 1 mm lip lets parts tunnel through. The feet's
  teeth sit 0.3 mm below the lip's underside in both.
- **Contact offset** of the parts 0.2 mm (PhysX), so the 0.8 mm blade goes into the 2.4 mm slot.
- **Friction:** rail steel 0.3, block housing polyamide 0.35, latch parts on the lips 0.25, screwdriver handle 0.9
  (rubber), ducts and wires 0.4, the table and robot fingers PhysX default 0.5.
- **The block's velocity** (for "still") comes from its pose change: the latch joints' armature skews PhysX's.
